Case 05 · Kissflow · Marketing UX · 2016–2022

Five products. A menu that changed on every page. Pogo-sticking cut 60%.

My role
Marketing UX Researcher & Designer: research, IA, wireframes, measurement
Team

Tools

She mapped every trail.
01 · What the research showed

People were clicking things that weren't links.

I set up menu-click events, then read navigation summaries, pogo-sticking reports, heatmaps and session recordings from launch day. Desktop and mobile were researched separately; tablet was set aside at 2% of traffic.

1Clicks on unclickable elements.
2The main menu changed from page to page, so people lost their way back.
3"Pricing" appeared twice, and nothing showed which page you were on.
4A dropdown that broke default behaviour confused the product offering.
02 · Hypothesis

If a product page shows only that product's menu (Overview, Features, Pricing, Customers), drops Resources and Partners, and lowers the weight of "All products" and the logo, then more people will stay and explore within the product.

Primary: navigation within the productSecondary: pogo-sticking
Borrow familiar patterns
Competitor and multi-brand navigation, following Jakob's Law.
Desktop wireframes
3–5 options, one direction
Shown to stakeholders. The brand home lost its second-level nav, and every product got equal weight instead of highlighting Digital Workplace.
Thumb-zone mobile
Built around the thumb zone. 65% of mobile traffic landed on blogs.

05 · What I learned

Decide the launch and analysis plan before you ship, or data gaps will cost you weeks. Choosing what you won't do is half the job. And stand your ground for usability when a deadline pushes it out.
